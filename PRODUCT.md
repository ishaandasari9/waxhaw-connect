# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Residents of Waxhaw, North Carolina, across all ages, abilities, backgrounds, income levels, languages, and levels of digital confidence. People may arrive while casually exploring local opportunities, planning for their household, or urgently trying to locate help. The experience must support families, children and teens, adults, seniors, newcomers, caregivers, and residents with disabilities without treating any group as secondary.

## Product Purpose

Create one trustworthy, welcoming place where Waxhaw residents can discover community organizations, public programs, essential services, events, and other local resources. Success means people can quickly understand what is available, find resources relevant to their circumstances, distinguish urgent help from everyday services, and confidently take a next step. The product should be strong enough to demonstrate professional planning, evidence-based design, responsive behavior, accessibility, useful interactivity, and measurable outcomes in the FBLA Website Design event.

## Brand Personality

Welcoming, trustworthy, and contemporary. The voice is calm, inclusive, plain-spoken, and respectful. It should feel locally grounded and optimistic while remaining credible enough for high-stakes information.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble a cluttered government directory, a tourism brochure, a childish school project, or a generic card-grid template. Avoid bureaucratic language, excessive decoration, flashy effects that obstruct tasks, inaccessible color choices, overly dense pages, and visual choices that stereotype demographic groups.

## Design Principles

1. **A useful answer before a beautiful page.** Make the next step obvious and keep critical information easy to scan.
2. **One community, many circumstances.** Support all ages and demographics through inclusive language, flexible discovery paths, and representation without stereotypes.
3. **Trust is designed and documented.** Show source provenance, freshness, eligibility, contact details, and clear boundaries around emergency information.
4. **Progressive guidance over directory overload.** Offer strong search and filtering for confident users plus guided discovery for people who do not know what to search for.
5. **Accessibility is a core interaction model.** Keyboard access, assistive-technology semantics, readable content, reduced motion, forgiving controls, and cross-device resilience are built in from the beginning.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 Level AA. Support keyboard-only navigation, screen readers, visible focus states, color-blind-safe contrast, zoom and text resizing, large touch targets, plain language, semantic landmarks and headings, descriptive labels, useful alternative text, reduced motion, and accessible validation and status announcements. Separate emergency assistance from non-emergency resources and avoid relying on color, icons, audio, hover, or device size alone to communicate meaning.
